# The 12 Static Ad Formats

Identification guide. Use to classify a reference, then to pick the next variant.

Grounded in a study of 67,852 ads across 106 brands. Statics are 55.6% of all ads run, 64.8% for DTC. Healthy accounts keep 6-8 of these formats live at once.

---

## 1. Product Hero
**Looks like:** the product large and clean on a neutral or brand-colored background. Minimal text.
**Layout signature:** product centered or slightly off-center, occupying 50-70% of frame. Headline in a band above or below.
**Awareness:** product-aware, most-aware. Weak on cold traffic.
**Copy pattern:** name the product plus one benefit, or the offer alone.
**Wins when:** the product is visually distinctive, or the audience already knows the category.
**Fails when:** run cold. Nobody stops for a thing they have no reason to want yet.

## 2. Social Proof / Testimonial
**Looks like:** a customer quote, star rating, or a screenshotted review. Often a face.
**Layout signature:** quote dominates the upper two-thirds, attribution beneath, product small in a corner or absent.
**Awareness:** solution-aware, product-aware.
**Copy pattern:** first-person, specific, slightly awkward. Polished testimonials read as fake.
**Wins when:** the purchase is high-consideration. Beats benefit-copy above roughly the 80 dollar mark.
**Fails when:** the quote is generic ("Love it!") or too clean to be real.

## 3. Before & After
**Looks like:** split frame, two states, usually left-right or top-bottom.
**Layout signature:** hard divider. Labels small. The contrast between panels is the whole ad.
**Awareness:** problem-aware.
**Copy pattern:** minimal. The image argues; the copy timestamps it ("6 weeks").
**Wins when:** the transformation is visible and honest.
**Fails when:** it shames the "before". Treat the before with empathy and frame the after as confidence, not correction. Also the single most policy-risky format - see `meta-specs.md`.

## 4. Feature Callout
**Looks like:** product with arrows, lines, or labels pointing to specific attributes.
**Layout signature:** product centered, 3-5 annotations radiating out, even spacing.
**Awareness:** solution-aware, product-aware.
**Copy pattern:** short noun phrases, one per callout. No sentences.
**Wins when:** the product's value is in details a photo alone will not show.
**Fails when:** more than five callouts. It becomes a diagram nobody reads.

## 5. Us vs. Them / Comparison
**Looks like:** two columns, or a table of checks and crosses.
**Layout signature:** vertical split or grid. Your side gets the accent color and the checks; the other side is grey.
**Awareness:** solution-aware. The buyer is choosing, not discovering.
**Copy pattern:** parallel phrasing across rows. Each row is one dimension.
**Wins when:** there is a real category incumbent to define yourself against.
**Fails when:** the named competitor is identifiable - that is a legal and platform problem. Compare against a category ("ordinary <x>"), never a brand.

## 6. UGC-Native / Screenshot
**Looks like:** a selfie, a text message thread, a Notes-app screenshot, a comment section.
**Layout signature:** deliberately mimics a native interface. Slightly imperfect framing.
**Awareness:** problem-aware, solution-aware.
**Copy pattern:** conversational, lowercase, typo-adjacent. Reads as a person, not a brand.
**Wins when:** it earns a half-second of "is this an ad?" That pause is the whole mechanism.
**Fails when:** it is too well-produced, or the brand logo appears prominently. Both break the illusion instantly.

## 7. Grid / Collage / Mood Board
**Looks like:** multiple images tiled, Pinterest style.
**Layout signature:** 2x2, 3x3, or a ragged collage. No single focal point by design.
**Awareness:** unaware, problem-aware. Sells a world, not an item.
**Copy pattern:** minimal or none. A single line overlaid, or nothing.
**Wins when:** the brand sells taste, aesthetic, or range.
**Fails when:** the tiles have no common thread, or there are more than nine.

## 8. Offer / Promo
**Looks like:** big typography, a number, a code, urgency.
**Layout signature:** the offer is the largest element on the canvas. Product secondary.
**Awareness:** most-aware. Retargeting and returning customers.
**Copy pattern:** number, deadline, code. Nothing else.
**Wins when:** the audience already wanted it and needed a reason to move today.
**Fails when:** run cold, or when discounting is the only angle a brand ever runs.

## 9. Ugly / Lo-Fi / MS Paint
**Looks like:** handwritten arrows, crude circles, screenshot with scribbles, deliberately unpolished.
**Layout signature:** breaks every design rule on purpose. Asymmetric, cramped, high-contrast.
**Awareness:** problem-aware.
**Copy pattern:** blunt, direct, often a single scrawled sentence.
**Wins when:** the feed is full of polished ads. It works by contrast with its surroundings, not on its own merits.
**Fails when:** the brand's category punishes cheapness - luxury, medical, financial.

## 10. Data / Stat Callout
**Looks like:** one large number dominating the frame.
**Layout signature:** the statistic is the hero, occupying the visual center. Everything else supports.
**Awareness:** unaware, problem-aware. Good for cold.
**Copy pattern:** the number, then one line of context. Source line small.
**Wins when:** the number is surprising and defensible.
**Fails when:** the stat is unverifiable. Meta rejects unsupported claims and so do buyers.

## 11. Meme / Culture
**Looks like:** a recognizable internet format, repurposed.
**Layout signature:** whatever the meme's native layout is. Fidelity to the format matters more than brand polish.
**Awareness:** unaware. Pure attention play.
**Copy pattern:** the meme's own grammar. Getting it slightly wrong is worse than not trying.
**Wins when:** the brand's audience is native to that culture.
**Fails when:** the reference is stale, or the brand is visibly trying. Also risks copyright on the source image.

## 12. Listicle / "Reasons Why"
**Looks like:** a numbered list of 3-5 benefits or ingredients, text-heavy.
**Layout signature:** vertical stack, consistent left alignment, numbers or icons as anchors. Product small, often bottom.
**Awareness:** problem-aware, solution-aware.
**Copy pattern:** parallel structure. Each item the same shape and roughly the same length.
**Wins when:** the product has several distinct reasons to buy and no single dominant one.
**Fails when:** more than five items, or items of wildly uneven length. Both kill the scan.

## Picking the next variant

Vary one axis at a time so the result is readable:

| Axis | Options |
|---|---|
| Format | the 12 above |
| Visual style | polished brand / lo-fi UGC / editorial |
| Angle | pain / aspiration / social proof / curiosity / humor / education |
| Offer | free gift / percent off / dollar off / BOGO / none |
| Persona | each distinct audience gets its own creative |

Cold traffic rule: problem-aware beats product-first. Name the pain before naming the product.

When something wins, do not make five near-copies of it. Carry the winning *message* into a genuinely different format.
